01 / DIFFUSION PUZZLE

Diffusion, one piece at a time

Noise moves each piece away from its place. The score points toward more likely positions. Two reverse processes bring the pieces back into a coherent picture.

What moves a piece?

The blue arrow is the score. In this model, it points from the current position x toward the reference position μ. As noise decreases, the same displacement produces a stronger score.

Equations and numerical accuracy

Each piece position has a Gaussian distribution around its known reference μ. Positions are independent, with σ₀ = 0.004. The model illustrates diffusion of positions; it does not infer the image or discover the solution to an unknown puzzle.

pt(x) = 𝒩(x; μ, v(t)I),　v(t) = σ₀² + 2Dt
st(x) = ∇ log pt(x) = −(x − μ)/v(t)

Differentiating log pₜ = constant − ‖x − μ‖²/(2v(t)) gives the score above. The arrow displays its direction, with length capped for readability.

ODE: dx = D st(x) dr
SDE: dx = 2D st(x) dr + √(2D) dBr,　r = T − t

With an exact score and initial samples from pT, both continuous-time processes have distribution pₜ at the same remaining time. Their individual paths differ. Manually moving a piece changes the initial distribution, so this equality no longer applies to that edited sample.

· ODE error against the exact solution

Default: exact Gaussian updates. Let z = x − μ, v = v(t), v′ = v(t − Δr), and q = v′/v. The ODE scales z by √q. The SDE draws from its exact reverse conditional distribution:

ODE: z′ = √q z
SDE: z′ = qz + √[v′(1 − q)] ξ,　ξ ∼ 𝒩(0, I)

Steps are spaced uniformly in log v(t). Euler and Euler–Maruyama approximate the continuous equations; increasing the step count reduces discretization error. Slider frames reuse the same ε for comparison and are not a Brownian trajectory. A small final variance remains: no pieces are snapped to the grid.

This puzzle uses a known reference layout to make the score visible. The experiments below retain the spectral view of diffusion and the distinction between inversion and generation.

02 / FORWARD DIFFUSION

Spectral attenuation

Start with a probability density wrapped around a circle, with 0 and 2π meeting at the same point. Brownian motion spreads its mass. The evolving density follows the heat equation.

∂pt∂t=∂2pt∂θ2,p0(θ)=1+∑k=124[akcos(kθ)+bksin(kθ)](1)

Each Fourier mode is an eigenfunction of the heat operator: ∂²θ cos(kθ) = −k² cos(kθ). Its coefficient decays by hₖ(t) = exp(−k²t). Doubling the frequency quadruples the decay rate. Throughout, D = 1 and densities are measured relative to dθ/(2π).

Derivation of the regularized inverse

For each real coefficient, differentiate (hₖc − yₖ)² + λc² with respect to c. The normal equation gives the filter below. Larger λ limits noise amplification while increasing signal shrinkage.

2hk(hkĉk − yk) + 2λĉk = 0
gk = hk / (hk² + λ),　ĉk = gkyk
yk = hkck + ηk,　ηk ∼ Uniform[−ε, ε]

Why the cross term vanishes

The noise has zero mean, and the source coefficients are fixed. The expected bias–noise cross term is therefore zero. Under dθ/(2π), each sine and cosine has squared norm 1/2; this accounts for the factor in the risk formula.

05 / FROM INVERSION TO GENERATION

From density to samples

So far, the unknown was a density recovered from its measured coefficients. Score-based generation starts with samples instead. The score sₜ(θ) = ∂θ log pₜ(θ) guides particles through a prescribed family of densities. Here it follows directly from the Fourier expansion.

st(θ)=∑kke−k2t[−aksin(kθ)+bkcos(kθ)]pt(θ)(6)

Write reverse time as r = T − t. The reverse of forward Brownian motion dθ = √2 dWₜ and its probability flow ODE then become:

REVERSE SDE
dΘᵣ = 2sT−r(Θᵣ) dr + √2 dBᵣ

A score-driven drift and fresh Brownian noise shape each path.

Each starting point determines a path. The score coefficient is half that of the SDE.

06 / MODEL & REFERENCES

A note on the model

Analytic quantities

The puzzle uses an independent Gaussian model with a known reference layout and analytic score. Fourier evolution, inverse filters, and expected error in the spectral experiments are also analytic.

Numerical quantities

Coefficient measurement noise and Brownian sampling noise are separate. Reconstructions retain negative values. Particle generation uses numerical integration, 512 samples, and 32 histogram bins.
