01 / FORWARD DIFFUSION

Spectral attenuation

Start with a probability density wrapped around a circle, with 0 and 2π meeting at the same point. Brownian motion spreads its mass. The evolving density follows the heat equation.

∂pt∂t=∂2pt∂θ2,p0(θ)=1+∑k=124[akcos(kθ)+bksin(kθ)](1)

Each Fourier mode is an eigenfunction of the heat operator: ∂²θ cos(kθ) = −k² cos(kθ). Its coefficient decays by hₖ(t) = exp(−k²t). Doubling the frequency quadruples the decay rate. Throughout, D = 1 and densities are measured relative to dθ/(2π).

Derivation of the regularized inverse

For each real coefficient, differentiate (hₖc − yₖ)² + λc² with respect to c. The normal equation gives the filter below. Larger λ limits noise amplification while increasing signal shrinkage.

2hk(hkĉk − yk) + 2λĉk = 0
gk = hk / (hk² + λ),　ĉk = gkyk
yk = hkck + ηk,　ηk ∼ Uniform[−ε, ε]

04 / JIGSAW POSITION DIFFUSION

Diffusion on a jigsaw graph

A jigsaw represents a structured position space. Each piece has a two-dimensional position, and a grid graph connects neighbouring pieces. Gaussian diffusion perturbs the positions; an exact score guides the reverse process.

The reference layout μ and its graph define the prior. This experiment isolates the geometry of diffusion using a known layout; it does not infer an unknown solution from image content. Its parameters are independent of the Fourier experiments above.

05 / FROM INVERSION TO GENERATION

From density to samples

So far, the unknown was a density recovered from its measured coefficients. Score-based generation starts with samples instead. The score sₜ(θ) = ∂θ log pₜ(θ) guides particles through a prescribed family of densities. Here it follows directly from the Fourier expansion.

st(θ)=∑kke−k2t[−aksin(kθ)+bkcos(kθ)]pt(θ)(6)

Write reverse time as r = T − t. The reverse of forward Brownian motion dθ = √2 dWₜ and its probability flow ODE then become:

REVERSE SDE
dΘᵣ = 2sT−r(Θᵣ) dr + √2 dBᵣ

A score-driven drift and fresh Brownian noise shape each path.

PROBABILITY FLOW ODE
dΘᵣ = sT−r(Θᵣ) dr

Each starting point determines a path. The score coefficient is half that of the SDE.

06 / MODEL & REFERENCES

A note on the model

Analytic quantities

Fourier evolution, inverse filters, expected error, and the jigsaw score are analytic. Initial Fourier coefficients are bounded to ensure positive densities.

Numerical quantities

Coefficient measurement noise and Brownian sampling noise are separate. Reconstructions retain negative values. Particle generation uses numerical integration, 512 samples, and 32 histogram bins.
