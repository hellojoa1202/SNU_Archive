1. THE FORWARD PROCESS

The forward SDE and its marginals

We use the variance-exploding SDE from the lecture, dx = dw, so f = 0, g = 1 and σ²(t) = t. The perturbation kernel is a Gaussian around the starting point, and the marginal pt averages it over the data.

p_t(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal N\big(x_t ;\, x_0,\ [\,\htmlData{link=time}{\sigma^2(t) - \sigma^2(0)}\,]\, I\big)

Computing the kernel, variance exploding · Score SDE notes, p. 18.

p_t(x_t) = \htmlData{link=mixture}{\int p_t(x_t \mid x_0)\, p_{\mathrm{data}}(x_0)\, dx_0}

Marginal densities · Score SDE notes, p. 21. For our mixture the integral is the same mixture with variance τ² + t.

The modes are 0.22 apart, so they merge once √(τ² + t) reaches about half that, near t = 0.01. By t = 0.05 the density is one blob. SNR(t) = 1 / t says how much of x0 is still in xt; it never reaches zero, which is the first approximation waiting to happen.

2. GENERATION

Reverse SDE and probability flow ODE share the marginals

Both equations pass through every pt: their Fokker–Planck equations are solved by the forward marginals. The reverse SDE uses the full score and fresh noise; the probability flow ODE uses half the score and no noise. Nothing is said about individual trajectories.

d\bar x(t) = \big[\, f(\bar x(t), t) - \htmlData{link=score}{g(t)^2\, \nabla \log p_t(\bar x(t))} \,\big]\, dt + \htmlData{link=randomness}{g(t)\, d\bar w(t)}, \qquad \bar x(T) \sim p_T

Reverse-time SDE · Score SDE notes, p. 63.

\frac{d\tilde x(t)}{dt} = f(\tilde x(t), t) - \htmlData{link=score}{\frac{1}{2}\, g(t)^2\, \nabla \log p_t(\tilde x(t))}

Probability flow ODE · Score SDE notes, p. 73.

They are the two ends of one family. For every η ≥ 0 the process below has the same marginals: the extra ½ η² g² of score in the drift cancels the extra noise exactly. η = 1 is the reverse SDE, η = 0 the probability flow ODE.

d\bar x(t) = \Big[\, f - \htmlData{link=eta}{\frac{1+\eta^2}{2}}\, g^2\, \htmlData{link=score}{\nabla \log p_t} \,\Big]\, dt + \htmlData{link=randomness}{\eta\, g\, d\bar w(t)}, \qquad \eta \ge 0

One marginal family, many processes · Score SDE notes, p. 78.

Annealing: the same equation as a cooling crystal

AUTO + MANUAL

Molten metal cooled slowly crystallises: every atom finds a lattice site. Cooled too fast it is quenched, with atoms stranded between sites and sites left empty. Near a site, pt is a Gaussian of width τ² + t, which is a harmonic well at temperature τ² + t, so the forward time is the temperature: t = 1 is the melt and t = 0 the cold crystal. Read this way, the score is the force of the lattice, η g dw̄ is the thermal motion, the time grid is the cooling schedule, and the prior is how hot the melt was to begin with. The lecture's annealed Langevin dynamics is this picture made literal.

3. THE THREE APPROXIMATIONS

Prior, score, discretization: one substitution at a time

A sampler starts from the prior instead of pT, evaluates a trained network sϕ× instead of ∇ log pt, and takes finite steps. The lecture writes the generative equations with these replacements and notes that they are the only three approximations.

\begin{aligned} d\bar x &= \big[\, f(\bar x, t) - g^2(t)\, \htmlData{link=score-term}{s_{\phi^\times}(\bar x, t)} \,\big]\, dt + g(t)\, d\bar w, \qquad & \htmlData{link=start}{\bar x(T) \sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}} \\[2pt] \frac{d\tilde x}{dt} &= f(\tilde x, t) - \frac{1}{2}\, g^2(t)\, \htmlData{link=score-term}{s_{\phi^\times}(\tilde x, t)}, & \htmlData{link=start}{\tilde x(T) \sim p_{\mathrm{prior}}} \end{aligned}

From the oracle to the model · Score SDE notes, p. 86.

x_{t-\Delta t} \leftarrow x_t - \big[\, f(x_t, t) - g^2(t)\, \htmlData{link=score-term}{s_{\phi^\times}(x_t, t)} \,\big]\, \htmlData{link=grid}{\Delta t} + g(t)\, \htmlData{link=grid}{\sqrt{\Delta t}}\, z

Euler–Maruyama step · Score SDE notes, p. 87. The Euler step of the ODE drops the noise and halves the score term; Heun adds a second evaluation per step (appendix p. 16).

\htmlData{link=start}{D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(p_T \,\|\, \mathcal N(0, P(T)\, I)\big) \le \frac{1}{2}\, \mathrm{SNR}(T)\, \mathbb E\lVert x_0\rVert^2}, \qquad \mathrm{SNR}(T) = \frac{E(0 \to T)^2}{P(T)}

The residue is the signal-to-noise ratio · Score SDE notes, p. 23, 26. Here E ≡ 1, P(T) = T, so SNR(T) = 1 / T.

The prior and the grid are reproduced exactly as a sampler makes them. The toy has no trained network, so two controlled errors stand in for it: a smoothing δ, which evaluates the exact score at t + δ, and a constant bias b. The sampler then sees ∇ log pt+δ(x) + b.

  • Prior. The bound gives 0.2 nats at T = 1; the true KL is 0.02. The ODE is a bijection and carries the start error to t = 0: at T = 0.25 a fifth of the mass lands in the wrong mode. The SDE forgets its start and shows half of that.
  • Score. A bias moves mass between modes and leaves their width alone; smoothing widens the modes and leaves the masses alone. The SDE is more sensitive to a bias than the ODE, by the factor (1 + η²)/2 in front of the score; noise cannot average out an error that points the same way every step.
  • Discretization. ‖∇ log pt‖ grows like 1 / (τ² + t), so a uniform grid arrives at the last steps, where the modes form, with a step that is too large. With few evaluations Euler beats Heun, whose predictor overshoots the narrow modes; Heun wins from about 256 evaluations. Euler–Maruyama also needs √Δt ≪ τ in its last step.

Three substitutions, two kinds of damage.Prior and bias move mass between modes; smoothing and a coarse grid widen them. The next section sweeps each knob.

Heun is worst below 32 evaluations and best from 256; Euler–Maruyama approaches the exact width from above.

The budget is not additive: mass moved to the wrong mode is not recovered by more steps, and a grid too coarse at the end is not repaired by a longer forward time. The first metric says where the mass went, the second how tightly it gathered.

5. THE SAME THREE, ELSEWHERE

Flow matching is a probability flow ODE

With a Gaussian source the conditional velocity of flow matching is an affine function of the conditional score, and averaging over the posterior makes the marginal velocity a drift plus a multiple of ∇ log pt. With the matched schedule αs = bT−s, σs = aT−s the flow-matching ODE and the probability flow ODE agree on (0, T).

v_t(x) = \htmlData{link=fm-drift}{\frac{\dot b_t}{b_t}\, x} + \htmlData{link=fm-score}{a_t^2 \Big( \frac{\dot b_t}{b_t} - \frac{\dot a_t}{a_t} \Big) \nabla \log p_t(x)}

The marginal velocity is a marginal score · Flow-based notes, p. 64; identification on the open interval, p. 69, 74.

So a flow-matching sampler is a probability flow ODE sampler and makes the same three substitutions.

  • The prior becomes exact, since b₀ = 0 puts the interpolant at pprior at t = 0. The cost moves into the coefficients of the matched diffusion, which diverge at that endpoint.
  • The score error becomes a velocity error. Conditional flow matching regresses on ȧtx₀ + ḃtxT, with the same conditional-expectation argument as denoising score matching.
  • The discretization is unchanged: conditional paths are straight, marginal trajectories are curved, and the solver still follows a curve.

Scope

The crystal picture is exact as far as it goes: near a site pt is a Boltzmann weight in a harmonic well at temperature τ² + t. It says nothing about the forces between sites, which in the sampler are set by the mixture and in a metal by chemistry. Bias and smoothing stand in for the unknown error of a trained network because their effects can be predicted and checked. The time grid is uniform.

Model and sources

Toy: 48 Gaussians, τ = 0.06, pitch 0.22, forward dx = dw. Closed forms for pt, ∇ log pt, cell masses and the x-marginal; KL by quadrature. Samplers: the η-family with Euler–Maruyama for η > 0 and Euler or Heun for η = 0, seeded. Tests (node --test) check the score against finite differences, the heat equation, the KL bound, the cell masses and the marginal alignment of the family.
