  • What stays fixed: at a fixed position and time, the variance does not depend on the candidate velocity. Moving the candidate changes only its error from the mean.

  • Learning: equality of the objectives up to a constant does not guarantee successful optimization. This demo evaluates the mean analytically and trains no network.

Build displacement out of signed area

Interaction

Euler contributes rectangles; Heun contributes trapezoids built from its stage evaluations. Their total signed area equals each method’s displacement.

Endpoint error versus field evaluations, on logarithmic axes. The active budget is highlighted. Reference: Heun with 4,096 steps, checked against 2,048 steps. Refinement is a consistency check, not a certified error bound.

  • Cost: Euler evaluates once per step, Heun twice. Equal evaluation counts compare their computational budgets directly.
  • Accuracy: under the smoothness assumptions in the notes, Euler has first-order global error and Heun second-order global error. The advantage at a particular budget still depends on the field and path.
  • Read carefully: deviation from an assigned destination is not the same as numerical error against the reference trajectory.

Model and course sources

  • Lecture equations: every displayed equation is transcribed from the supplied notes. Source captions name the original slide and PDF page. No additional energy, assignment-cost, or closed-form probability equations are introduced.
  • Numerical example: equally weighted translated Gaussian components of fixed width 0.16; a shared offset at paired endpoints. This tractable example and its interface are project constructions.
  • Evaluation: densities, conditional means, and conditional losses are computed directly. Particle trajectories use 1,024-step Heun; tracked references use 4,096-step Heun. No network is trained.
