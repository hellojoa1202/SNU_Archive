DIFFUSION DETECTIVE / FLOW MATCHING

Same destination.
Different flows.

Design the connections. Build a velocity field. Trace what actually moves.

A coupling assigns destinations to starting points. A flow sees only the current position and time. This distinction changes the paths—even when the endpoint distributions stay the same.

CH4 · Conditional Flow Matching → CH3 · continuity equation → numerical integration

A coupling determines conditional paths; conditional expectation turns those paths into a marginal velocity; the continuity equation identifies the transported law; a numerical solver approximates the resulting trajectories. The same twelve-component model makes each link explicit.

The smooth probability model behind the pieces

To obtain a smooth field, each center carries a Gaussian component of fixed width τ = 0.16. For a component i, the same offset Z is carried from Aᵢ to Bπ(i). The puzzle outlines are identity markers, not probability contours.

I ∼ Uniform{1,…,12},　Z ∼ 𝒩(0,I₂),　I ⫫ Z
Xt = μI(t) + τZ,　dXt/dt = uI
qi,t(x) = 𝒩(x; μi(t), τ²I₂),　pt(x) = ¹⁄₁₂ ∑i qi,t(x)

Changing the permutation π leaves p₀ and p₁ unchanged. Dragging a starting center changes p₀. The time convention throughout is source at t = 0, destination at t = 1.

More precisely, the coupling is the joint law of (Aᵢ + τZ, Bπ(i) + τZ), averaged over i. Keeping the offset shared makes every component translate without changing its width. It also gives a closed-form velocity target for every sampled pair.

What changing the coupling actually changes

K(π) = E‖U‖² = ¹⁄₁₂ ∑i ‖Bπ(i) − Ai‖²
πmin ∈ arg minπ ∈ S₁₂ K(π)

The comparison button minimizes this quadratic cost over all permutations of the twelve destinations. It changes the assignment while retaining the current source centers. The objective is the mean squared speed of the conditional paths; it is not the kinetic energy of the marginal ODE. Those quantities differ whenever conditional directions disagree.

Compare the original pairing with the minimum-cost pairing at t ≈ 0.5. Inspect how the intermediate density and local directions change. Lower assignment cost alone does not guarantee lower error for every finite-budget solver.

02 / CONDITIONAL VELOCITY

Merge the plans into one velocity field

The field receives (x,t), not the hidden component identity I. Near one location, several components can contribute different velocities. Their posterior-weighted average is the velocity available to the field.

ri(x,t) = qi,t(x) / ∑jqj,t(x)
v*(x,t) = E[U | Xt = x] = ∑i ri(x,t)ui

Click anywhere to probe the field. Thin arrows: local field. Colored arrows: rᵢuᵢ for the three highest-weight components. Dark arrow: the sum over all twelve. All arrows use the same length scale.

In “Edit prediction” mode, drag to set the purple candidate vector from the probe. Arrow keys adjust it. The vector is limited to ±2.5 per coordinate. The candidate changes the regression error; it does not replace the exact field used by the transport experiment.

Conditional MSE
Excess error ‖f − v*‖²
Velocity variance C(x,t)

Training can use the individual velocity U, which is known for every sampled pair. The model still learns the conditional mean because the squared-error objective splits into a learnable term and a constant.

E[‖f − U‖² | x,t] = ‖f − v*(x,t)‖² + C(x,t)
C(x,t) = ∑i ri(x,t)‖ui − v*(x,t)‖²

At a fixed probe, the first term is controllable by the candidate f. The second is the conditional variance of the hidden velocity target. Setting f = v* removes the excess error but leaves C(x,t). A nonzero training loss can therefore coexist with the correct marginal velocity.

ℒCFM(θ) = E‖vθ(Xt,t) − U‖²
　= E‖vθ(Xt,t) − v*(Xt,t)‖² + C
C = E‖U − v*(Xt,t)‖²　(independent of θ)
Why the cross term vanishes
E[U − v*(Xt,t) | Xt,t] = 0
E⟨vθ − v*, v* − U⟩ = 0

Expand vθ − U = (vθ − v*) + (v* − U), then condition on (Xₜ,t). The residual has conditional mean zero. The equality holds for any predictor; attaining v* additionally requires sufficient model capacity and optimization. Here v* is evaluated analytically. Expectations average over t ∼ Uniform[0,1] and the sampled component and offset.

‖f − U‖² = ‖f − v*‖² + ‖v* − U‖²
　+ 2⟨f − v*, v* − U⟩
E[⟨f − v*, v* − U⟩ | Xt,t]
　= ⟨f − v*, E[v* − U | Xt,t]⟩ = 0

Direction cancellation connects the loss to the coupling

E[‖U‖² | x,t] = ‖v*(x,t)‖² + C(x,t)
K(π) = EXₜ∼pₜ‖v*(Xt,t)‖² + EXₜ∼pₜC(Xt,t)

The identity follows from the same orthogonal decomposition, now with f = 0. Opposing conditional velocities can have large individual magnitudes and a small average. The conditional variance measures the lost squared magnitude. The global identity holds at each fixed t in this translation model because the distribution of U is time-independent.

Change a pairing, return to the same probe and time, and compare the variance. The cost K(π) describes the entire coupling; C(x,t) describes one local ambiguity. Neither should be read as a standalone guarantee of sampler accuracy.

03 / TRANSPORT

Follow the field, not the assignment

Now follow dx/dt = v*(x,t) from the selected starting center. The field re-evaluates the direction along the trajectory. It has no access to the original assignment, so its path need not follow the planned line.

Drag the dashed vertical boundary horizontally to measure the region. The sample fraction fluctuates around the model probability; 96 samples are a visual check, not a proof of equality. X₁ here denotes the first spatial coordinate, not the terminal time.

The agreement is at the level of distributions. Each translated component satisfies a continuity equation. Summing them shows that the posterior-weighted velocity transports their mixture.

Since uᵢ is constant in space and time, differentiating qᵢ,t(x) = qᵢ,0(x − tuᵢ) gives ∂ₜqᵢ,t = −uᵢ · ∇qᵢ,t. The divergence of uᵢ vanishes. Multiplying the posterior mean by pₜ cancels the normalization in rᵢ, leaving exactly the component probability fluxes.

∂tqi,t = −∇·(qi,tui)
ptv* = ¹⁄₁₂∑iqi,tui
∂tpt + ∇·(ptv*) = 0

With the matching initial law and a well-posed ODE, the flow reproduces pₜ. This statement does not preserve component identities. Reaching the destination distribution therefore does not guarantee that each photo piece occupies its assigned slot.

Conservation can be checked on a region

Pt(X(1) ≤ c) = ¹⁄₁₂∑i Φ((c − μi(1)(t))/τ)
dPt/dt = −∫ pt(c,y)v*1(c,y,t)dy
　= −¹⁄₁₂∑i (ui(1)/τ) φ((c − μi(1)(t))/τ)

Φ and φ are the standard-normal CDF and density. Integrating the continuity equation over the half-plane turns a change in its probability into flux across its boundary. Positive dPₜ/dt means net mass enters the left region; a fixed target density at t = 1 does not make this intermediate flux independent of the coupling.

Why an assigned path can cross while an ODE path cannot

Different conditional components may occupy the same location at the same time while carrying different velocities. They retain their component identity. A well-posed marginal ODE assigns one velocity to each (x,t); uniqueness prevents distinct solutions from meeting at the same space-time point and then separating. Thus it transports the marginals without generally preserving the endpoint pairing. Paths may still cross geometrically at different times.

04 / NUMERICAL INTEGRATION

Compute the path with a finite budget

The exact velocity is now fixed. Only the numerical integration changes. Compare Euler and Heun with the same number of field evaluations; Heun uses two evaluations per step. The reference is a finer RK4 solve.

Along a trajectory, velocity changes both because time advances and because the particle enters a different part of the field. This material acceleration determines the first term missed by Euler.

a(x,t) = ∂tv*(x,t) + (Dxv*(x,t))v*(x,t)
x(t+h) = x(t) + hv*(x,t) + ½h²a(x,t) + O(h³)

Euler retains only the first-order term. Heun averages the initial velocity and a predicted endpoint velocity, recovering the second-order term. Under sufficient smoothness, their global errors scale as O(h) and O(h²), respectively. The comparison below uses the same field throughout.

Euler:　xk+1 = xk + hv*(xk,tk)
Heun:　x̃ = xk + hv*(xk,tk)
　xk+1 = xk + ½h[v*(xk,tk) + v*(x̃,tk+1)]

Changing the coupling changes the field and its trajectories. Changing the solver changes how that same field is approximated. A missed assigned slot and a numerical integration error are different quantities; only the latter is measured here.

B evaluations:　hEuler = 1/B,　hHeun = 2/B

Equal steps would give Heun twice as many field evaluations. Equal NFE makes the computational comparison explicit, but the constants in the error estimates still depend on the field and the chosen trajectory. A lower assignment cost is not a proof that one method will win at a particular budget.

Model & sources

The four views share one coupling, selected piece, and clock. Twelve equally weighted components use τ = 0.16 throughout. Velocities and densities are analytic; trajectories are numerical. No network is trained. The simulation transports coordinates, not image pixels or physical interlocking pieces. The 96 transported samples use 512-step RK4; tracked center paths use 2,048 steps.

Checks cover: permutation invariance of p₀ and p₁; normalization of posterior weights; conditional loss and energy decompositions; finite-difference residuals of the continuity equation; half-plane probability flux; and Euler/Heun convergence. The minimum-cost assignment is exact over permutations of the twelve centers. Region probabilities use a normal-CDF approximation with absolute error below 10⁻⁷.
