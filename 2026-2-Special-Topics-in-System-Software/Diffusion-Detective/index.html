01 / FORWARD DIFFUSION

The fading spectrum

Start with a probability density wrapped around a circle, with 0 and 2π meeting at the same point. Brownian motion spreads its mass. The evolving density follows the heat equation.

∂pt∂t=∂2pt∂θ2,p0(θ)=1+∑k=124[akcos(kθ)+bksin(kθ)](1)

Each Fourier mode is an eigenfunction of the heat operator: ∂²θ cos(kθ) = −k² cos(kθ). Its coefficient decays by hₖ(t) = exp(−k²t). Doubling the frequency quadruples the decay rate. Throughout, D = 1 and densities are measured relative to dθ/(2π).

02 / THE INVERSE PROBLEM

Running it backward

For an exact observation, division by hₖ recovers the original coefficient. Add a small measurement error ηₖ, and the same division amplifies the error along with the signal. Here cₖ stands for either a sine or a cosine coefficient.

03 / BIAS–VARIANCE TRADEOFF

Choosing what to recover

A filter trades fidelity for stability. With gain gₖ, the reconstruction error splits into bias from shrinking the signal and noise passed through the filter.

ck^−ck=(gkhk−1)ckbias+gkηknoise amplification(3)

Each real coefficient receives independent Uniform[−ε, ε] noise, with variance ε²/3. Parseval’s identity turns coefficient errors into density error, giving an exact bias–variance decomposition.

04 / IDENTIFIABILITY PUZZLES

Two small mysteries

The source is hidden now. One of three candidates produced each observation through diffusion and bounded measurement error. Which candidates still fit the evidence? Each puzzle has its own observation, independent of the controls above.

r(c)=maxk{|yka−hkak|,|ykb−hkbk|}≤ε(5)

A candidate fits when every coefficient residual is within ε. Multiple fits leave the source ambiguous under this candidate set and error bound. With exact coefficients, the finite Fourier model remains invertible; finite precision is what makes the distinction difficult.

05 / FROM INVERSION TO GENERATION

From density to samples

So far, the unknown was a density recovered from its measured coefficients. Score-based generation starts with samples instead. The score sₜ(θ) = ∂θ log pₜ(θ) guides particles through a prescribed family of densities. Here it follows directly from the Fourier expansion.

st(θ)=∑kke−k2t[−aksin(kθ)+bkcos(kθ)]pt(θ)(6)

Write reverse time as r = T − t. The reverse of forward Brownian motion dθ = √2 dWₜ and its probability flow ODE then become:

REVERSE SDE
dΘᵣ = 2sT−r(Θᵣ) dr + √2 dBᵣ

A score-driven drift and fresh Brownian noise shape each path.

PROBABILITY FLOW ODE
dΘᵣ = sT−r(Θᵣ) dr

Each starting point determines a path. The score coefficient is half that of the SDE.

06 / MODEL & REFERENCES

A note on the model

Analytic quantities

The finite Fourier evolution, inverse filters, candidate residuals, and expected error are analytic. Coefficient bounds keep every source density positive.

Numerical quantities

Coefficient measurement noise and Brownian sampling noise are separate. Reconstructions retain negative values. Particle generation uses numerical integration, 512 samples, and 32 histogram bins.
