The Shaken
Puzzle Board.

Three approximations of a diffusion sampler, measured one at a time.

On paper the reverse-time SDE and the probability flow ODE are exact. A sampler replaces three things it does not have: the true terminal law, the true score, and continuous time. This page builds a toy where everything else is known in closed form, so that each replacement can be switched on alone and its effect on the generated distribution can be seen.

Two views of the same density share every control: the puzzle board and a contour map. The 48 pieces are 48 tracked samples, each carrying the tile of the slot it was drawn from; the dots are the remaining samples. A sampler is judged on where the mass goes, not on which piece lands where, and the two can disagree.

1. THE FORWARD PROCESS

Shaking the board

The data distribution is an equal mixture of 48 Gaussians, one per slot of an 8 × 6 board, each of width τ = 0.06. The forward process is the variance-exploding SDE dx = dw: zero drift, unit diffusion. Because the drift is affine, the marginal at time t is the same mixture with every component widened by t. Nothing has to be simulated to know it.

pt(x) = (1/K) Σk N(x ; μk, (τ² + t) I),    K = 48

Perturbation kernel and marginal density · Score SDE notes, PDF p. 18, 21 · with f = 0, g = 1 the Fokker–Planck equation is the heat equation ∂tp = ½ Δp (p. 34, 51).

2. GENERATION

Rolling back: one family of samplers

The reverse-time SDE and the probability flow ODE are the two ends of one line. For every η ≥ 0 the process below has the same marginals as the forward process: the extra score in the drift cancels exactly the extra diffusion that the noise adds to the Fokker–Planck equation. η = 1 is the reverse SDE, η = 0 the probability flow ODE.

dx̄ = [ f − (1 + η²)/2 g² ∇ log pt(x̄) ] dt + η g dw̄

One marginal family, many processes · Score SDE notes, PDF p. 78 · reverse-time SDE p. 63, probability flow ODE p. 73.

3. THE THREE APPROXIMATIONS

Three substitutions, one at a time

With f = 0 and g = 1 one backward step of the family is a single line. A sampler changes three things in it: where x starts, which score it reads, and how large the step h is. The lecture notes name these as the only three approximations, and this toy keeps every other quantity exact so that each can be measured alone.

xT ∼ N(0, T I),    x ← x + (1 + η²)/2 · s̃(x, t) · h + η √h z,   z ∼ N(0, I)

From the oracle to the model · Score SDE notes, PDF p. 86 (the three substitutions) · Euler–Maruyama step p. 87.

s̃(x, t) = ∇ log pt + δ(x) + b

Defined on this page: a learned score is a regression estimate (denoising score matching, p. 83–84) whose error is unknown; the blur δ and the tilt b are two controlled errors in its place.

2048 samples: the 48 pieces and 552 of the remaining dots are drawn. A piece lying between slots is blur; a piece in the wrong slot is moved mass; an empty slot is missing mass. Slot error asks where the mass went, spread asks how tightly it gathered. Small grey numbers are the values for 2048 exact draws from p₀, the floor of each metric.

  • Start. The lecture bounds the mismatch by the signal-to-noise ratio: KL(pT ‖ N(0, P(T) I)) ≤ ½ SNR(T) E‖x₀‖² (PDF p. 23, 26). Here SNR(T) = 1 / T and the bound is loose by an order of magnitude but has the right trend. The ODE is a bijection and carries whatever is wrong at T to t = 0; the SDE keeps adding noise and pulling back, which lets it forget its start.
  • Score. A tilt moves mass between slots and shifts the picture sideways; a blur keeps the slot frequencies and widens every well. The family multiplies the score, and hence the tilt, by (1 + η²)/2, so in this toy the SDE is more sensitive to a constant tilt than the ODE. The usual robustness argument for stochastic sampling concerns errors that fluctuate and average out, which a tilt does not.
  • Grid. The field is stiff: ‖∇ log pt‖ grows like 1 / (τ² + t), so the last steps near t = 0 are the hard ones. At equal evaluations Euler on the ODE is the most accurate up to about 128; Heun overshoots the wells on coarse grids and pays off only from 256 on; Euler–Maruyama needs √h ≪ τ in its last step or its own noise blurs the wells.

The budget is not additive. A tilt that moves mass to the wrong slot cannot be repaired by more steps, and a coarse grid cannot be repaired by a longer shake. The two substitutions that move mass (start, tilt) and the two that blur it (blur, grid) separate cleanly in the two metrics.

5. THE SAME THREE, ELSEWHERE

What flow matching changes, and what it does not

Flow matching never writes a score, yet for a Gaussian source the field it learns is one: the conditional velocity is an affine function of the conditional score, and averaging over the posterior turns it into the marginal score. With the matched schedule αs = bT−s, σs = aT−s the flow-matching ODE is a probability flow ODE on (0, T).

vt(x) = (ḃt / bt) x + at² (ḃt/bt − ȧt/at) ∇ log pt(x)

And the marginal velocity is a marginal score · Flow-based notes, PDF p. 64 · identification on the open interval p. 69, 74.

  • The start is no longer an approximation: the interpolant reaches the prior exactly at t = 0 because b₀ = 0. The price is paid by the matched diffusion, whose coefficients diverge at that endpoint; a diffusion cannot reach N(0, I) in finite time.
  • The score error becomes a velocity error. Conditional flow matching regresses on ȧtx₀ + ḃtxT and its minimiser is the marginal velocity, by the same conditional-expectation argument that makes denoising score matching work.
  • The grid is unchanged. Straight conditional paths do not make the marginal trajectories straight; the solver still follows a curve.

Where the analogy stops

A piece’s tile names where that one sample started, not where it must end: the sampler is judged on mass per slot, and even the exact ODE sends piece k to another slot. A slot holds a fraction of the mass, not one piece. The board changes shape on its own, blurring under the heat equation and sharpening under the reverse process; the contour view is the honest picture of that. Tilt and blur stand in for an unknown network error because their effects can be predicted and checked, not because they are typical. The time grid is uniform; non-uniform spacing is a standard remedy for the stiffness in 3 and is left out as outside the lecture material.

Model and sources

Toy: 48 Gaussians, τ = 0.06, slot pitch 0.22, forward dx = dw. Closed forms for pt, ∇ log pt, slot masses and the x-marginal; KL by 140 × 140 quadrature; no neural network. Samplers: the η-family with Euler–Maruyama for η > 0 and Euler or Heun for η = 0, seeded so every run replays. Tests (node --test) check the score against finite differences, the heat equation, the KL bound, slot masses against Monte Carlo, and marginal alignment of the family.
