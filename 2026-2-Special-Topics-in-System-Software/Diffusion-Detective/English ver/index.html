1. NEURAL ODE FLOWS

Likelihood needs the whole trajectory

A continuous normalizing flow transports points with an ODE. For maximum-likelihood training, the position and the accumulated density change must be solved together.

log pϕ(xT) = log pprior(x0) − ∫0T ∇·vϕ(xt,t) dt

Training: maximum likelihood · PDF p. 32

d/dt log pt(xt) = −∇·vt(xt)

How the density moves along a trajectory · PDF p. 21

Read the equation: the starting log density is corrected by the divergence accumulated along the moving point. Expansion lowers local density; compression raises it.

What remains: even one likelihood evaluation follows a trajectory. Can training use a target available at a single position and time?

2. FLOW MATCHING

Replace likelihood evaluation with velocity regression

Prescribe a probability path and its generating velocity. Flow Matching fits that velocity pointwise, so the objective contains no trajectory solve.

LFM(ϕ) = Et, xₜ∼pₜ ‖vϕ(xt,t) − vt(xt)‖²

Regress the velocity instead · PDF p. 39

Likelihood-based flowInitial state → ODE steps → accumulated density → objective

What remains: pointwise regression is useful only if its target can be obtained. Instead of evaluating the marginal velocity, can we use a target from one sampled endpoint pair?

3. CONDITIONAL FLOW MATCHING

Construct a target from an endpoint pair

Sample two paired endpoints, interpolate their positions, and differentiate that path. This gives a conditional target without integrating the marginal ODE.

xt = atx0 + btxT
a0 = bT = 1,　aT = b0 = 0

Condition on the endpoint pair · PDF p. 43

ut(xt; x0, xT) := ẋt = ȧtx0 + ḃtxT

The conditional velocity · PDF p. 46

The velocity that generates the family · PDF p. 47

The photograph identifies a conditional endpoint pair. A marginal ODE reproduces the distribution path under the stated regularity; its individual trajectories need not keep those labeled assignments.

LCFM(ϕ) = Et, (x₀,xT)∼π ‖vϕ(xt,t) − ut(xt;x0,xT)‖²

Certified, and out of reach · PDF p. 50

LCFM(ϕ) = LFM(ϕ) + C

The minimizer is the marginal velocity · PDF p. 52

The model sees position and time, while different endpoint pairs can supply different target velocities there. Squared-error regression recovers their conditional mean. The conditional and marginal objectives differ by a parameter-independent constant.

Model, scope, and sources

  • One shared model: twelve equally weighted Gaussian components, width 0.16, carry a shared offset between paired endpoints. Pairing and initial-position edits affect all scenes.
  • What is computed: analytic velocities and losses; Heun trajectories and a divergence integral. The toy field substitutes for a learned network. The comparison concerns objective construction, not measured training speed.
  • Numerical resolution: material grid and particle paths: 1,024 steps; tracked log-density trace: 2,048 steps. Solver reference: 4,096 steps.

Flow-based notes: “How the density moves along a trajectory” (PDF p. 21), “Training: maximum likelihood” (p. 32), “Regress the velocity instead” (p. 39), and conditional targets and objective equivalence (pp. 43, 46–47, 50–52).
