1. A COMMON STARTING POINT

From predicted noise to the score

DDPM predicts noise; NCSN predicts the score. At the optimal predictor, a known scale factor connects the two. The next question is how this quantity moves a distribution.

s⋆(x̃,σ) = −(1/σ) ε⋆(x̃,σ)

Here ε⋆ is the conditional mean noise, not the particular noise used to corrupt one sample. The score is a gradient of log density. We use an analytic score below so that sampling behavior can be isolated from estimation error.

2. DIFFERENT PROCESSES

A random path is not the distribution

The reverse SDE injects noise along its trajectory. Its probability flow ODE has no stepwise noise and half the score correction. With the exact score and matching initial law, both reproduce the same time marginals.

dx̄(t) = [f(x̄(t),t) − g²(t) ∇ log pt(x̄(t))] dt + g(t) dw̄(t)
dx̃(t)/dt = f(x̃(t),t) − ½ g²(t) ∇ log pt(x̃(t))

3. LEARN A GENERATING VELOCITY

From matching distributions to learning a velocity

Instead of fixing the path through forward diffusion, choose a probability path. Flow Matching regresses its marginal velocity. The difficulty is evaluating that target: conditional paths provide the next step.

3.1 CONDITIONAL FLOW MATCHING

Straight conditional paths, curved marginal paths

Sample a paired start and endpoint, then interpolate. These individual paths may cross. Their conditional mean velocity defines a different ODE: under regularity, it reproduces the same intermediate distributions without keeping each pair’s assignment.

xt = atx0 + btxT
a0 = bT = 1,　aT = b0 = 0

The velocity that generates the family · PDF p. 47

The puzzle separates identity from position. Changing a pairing preserves the endpoint densities but generally changes the intermediate ones. For any fixed pairing, its conditional paths and the marginal ODE reproduce the same time marginals under regularity; individual pieces need not reach their assigned slots along the ODE.

∂tpt + ∇·(pt vt) = 0

Why does the average work?

For a fixed endpoint coupling, conditional averaging yields a velocity satisfying the continuity equation for the interpolation density. The density evolution constrains probability transport, not the identity of each trajectory. Changing the coupling generally changes the interior probability path even when the endpoints remain fixed.

Conditional expectation is the squared-error projection of the conditional target onto functions of position and time. The residual conditional variance is independent of the predictor. Averaging this decomposition gives the FM/CFM objective identity; their parameter gradients agree whenever differentiation is justified.

SUPPLEMENT · LIKELIHOOD

Likelihood needs the whole trajectory

A continuous normalizing flow transports points with an ODE. For maximum-likelihood training, the position and the accumulated density change must be solved together.

log pϕ(xT) = log pprior(x0) − ∫0T ∇·vϕ(xt,t) dt

Training: maximum likelihood · PDF p. 32

d/dt log pt(xt) = −∇·vt(xt)

How the density moves along a trajectory · PDF p. 21

Read the equation: the starting log density is corrected by the divergence accumulated along the moving point. Expansion lowers local density; compression raises it.

Model, scope, and sources

  • Score experiment: three smooth Gaussian components, exact score, exact terminal sampling. Reverse SDE: 512 Euler–Maruyama steps; probability flow ODE: 512 Heun steps.
  • CFM experiment: 48 translated Gaussian components, width 0.16, share a Gaussian offset within each endpoint pair. The conditional and marginal displays use the same initial samples.
  • Scope: analytic targets replace trained networks. Equal marginals apply within each experiment, not between the diffusion and CFM probability paths. Numerical and finite-sample discrepancies are visible.
